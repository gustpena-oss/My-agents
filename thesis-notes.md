# Thesis notes (working file, in progress)

Source: *Charles H. Cooley y la estructura comunicativa de la vida humana* (Universidad Complutense de Madrid, Doctorado en Periodismo; directors Rodríguez Polo and Ruiz San Román; first part directed by López-Escobar). Spanish, about 121,000 words. Read in full on 2026-10-07 (bibliography excluded).

## Structure
- Part I (ch. 1-3): Cooley's biography, how his concept of communication formed (transport → communication; Spencer, Schäffle, Dewey, Darwin), and his evolutionary anthropology.
- Part II (ch. 4-6): the state of human evolution: lithic cultures (Olduvai, Dmanisi), anatomy (pelvis, brain, Broca/Wernicke), phylogeny of communication (Petitto, Chomsky vs. Tomasello).
- Part III (ch. 7-8): Cooley's "theoretical core," the Mead obituary and its rebuttal (Schubert), the return to Cooley's claims against the science, and applications (political philosophy, urbanism, participatory method, dialogue with China).

## Author's own claim (stated 2026-10-07)
- Strongest claim: based on the fossil record, it is possible to suggest that Cooley's conceptual base is scientifically sound, and this means a change of paradigm for the social sciences.
- All five contributions are claimed as the author's own: (1) test of Cooley against the fossil/anatomical record; (2) supplying the anthropological/ethological grounding Mead said was missing; (3) Cooley-Tomasello convergence; (4) "pedestal science" for communication theory (vs. Craig and Muller); (5) applied program.
- Refutable core, accepted by the author: Cooley's claim is that the human lineage's survival depends on socially transmitted, communicated culture. It would be weakened if the stone-tool record could be explained without cooperative social learning.

## Known issues to handle in articles

- Step from "compatible with the fossil record" to "paradigm change" is not argued in the thesis.
